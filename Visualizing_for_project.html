1 The Why, What, and How Behind This Project

Core Problem Statement: Building an AI that flags credit card fraud is tough because fraud is extremely rare (only 0.173% of transactions)[cite: 1]. But the bigger real-world challenge is that static models quietly fail over time because customer spending habits shift and fraudsters invent new tactics.

🎯 The "Why"

  • Accuracy is deceptive: With 99.827% legitimate transactions, an AI model that falls asleep and labels every transaction "Normal" achieves 99.83% accuracy while catching zero fraud[cite: 1].
  • Static models degrade: An AI trained on past data becomes outdated when spending spikes or fraud techniques evolve.
  • Business cost asymmetry: Missing a stolen card hurts the bank far more than sending a routine SMS verification to a real customer.

⚙️ The "What" & "How"

  • What we built: An end-to-end pipeline that trains a balanced fraud detector, tunes decision strictness to minimize financial loss, and continuously checks for data drift.
  • How it works:
    1. Sliced 284,807 transactions chronologically (first 70% past, last 30% future) to simulate live deployment[cite: 1].
    2. Balanced decision tree penalties using class_weight='balanced'.
    3. Tracked feature shifts between the past baseline and future transactions to trigger automated retraining[cite: 1].

3 The Reasoning Behind the 0.24 Optimal Threshold

Standard machine learning models use a default cutoff of 0.50 (they only flag a transaction if they are at least 50% sure it is fraud). In banking, this default causes heavy losses.

Total Cost = (10 × False Negatives) + (1 × False Positives)
  • Why False Negatives cost 10x more: Missing an actual fraudster ($FN$) causes direct chargebacks, stolen funds, and customer churn. A False Positive ($FP$) merely causes a minor inconvenience, like sending an OTP confirmation or quick SMS alert.
  • Why not 0.50? At 0.50, the model is too relaxed. It misses around 20 frauds ($20 \times 10 = 200$), creating high business costs.
  • Why 0.24 is the sweet spot: Lowering the threshold to 0.24 makes the AI more vigilant. It catches 85.7% of all frauds with only 5 false alarms, minimizing total financial loss to its lowest point ($115)[cite: 1].
  • Why not lower it further to 0.20 or 0.15? Because 99.8% of customers are innocent, dropping the bar to 0.15 creates a flood of false alarms (flagging 40+ innocent people). The $40 penalty for false alarms quickly outweighs the $10 saved on catching one extra thief.

4 Deconstructing the Drift Monitoring Report

Drift monitoring acts as a continuous smoke detector for the machine learning model in production[cite: 1]:

Metric Name Recorded Value Simple Meaning Operational Objective
Baseline In-Sample Drift 0.0030[cite: 1] The natural background noise measured between two random slices of the training data[cite: 1]. Establishes the reference line for what normal day-to-day data variation looks like[cite: 1].
Alert Threshold 0.1000[cite: 1] The predefined safety boundary[cite: 1]. Any drift score below 0.10 is considered safe. Prevents unnecessary alarms while guaranteeing a flag before the model decays severely[cite: 1].
Production Drift Score 0.1476[cite: 1] The actual standardized mean shift between historical training data and the incoming future transactions[cite: 1]. Quantifies how much customer behavior or fraud methods have drifted from the initial training data[cite: 1].
End Objective of the Drift Monitor: Because the production score (0.1476) breached the 0.1000 alert threshold, the system identified that the model was slipping (Recall had dropped from 85.7% to 77.8% and missed 24 frauds)[cite: 1]. The end objective was achieved: instead of failing silently in production, the monitor caught the shift and triggered an automated retraining workflow to restore performance[cite: 1].

5 Random Forest vs. Isolation Forest: Why Train Both?

We trained both models to compare a Supervised approach against an Unsupervised baseline[cite: 1]:

Dimension Random Forest (Supervised) Isolation Forest (Unsupervised)
PR-AUC Score 0.8560 (Production Grade)[cite: 1] 0.1548 (Poor Separation)[cite: 1]
How It Learns Trained with explicit labels (Class = 0 vs Class = 1). It learns what past fraud looks like. Trained with zero labels. It assumes anomalies are simply points that can be isolated in very few splits.
Analogy A detective studying files of known bank robberies. A security guard stopping anyone who looks unusual or stands apart from the crowd.
Why We Compared Both To prove that supervised learning is vastly superior when labeled fraud data is available (0.8560 vs 0.1548)[cite: 1]. Isolation Forest flags any rare transaction—including a legitimate customer buying an expensive flight at 2 AM—causing too many false alarms[cite: 1]. However, Isolation Forest remains valuable as a fallback tool when entering a completely new, unlabeled transaction stream[cite: 1].
